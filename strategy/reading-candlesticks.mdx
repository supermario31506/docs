Candlestick charts pack a surprising amount of information into every block. In Mario Nintendo, the chart updates in real time — one new candle every second — giving you an instant visual history of SILVER's price action. Once you know how to read the individual pieces, patterns across multiple candles begin to tell a story about where the price might be heading next.

## Anatomy of a Single Candle

### Body (Open to Close)

The thick rectangular **body** of each candle represents the price range between where the interval *opened* and where it *closed*.

- **Green body** — the price closed **higher** than it opened. Buyers were in control during that second (bullish).
- **Red body** — the price closed **lower** than it opened. Sellers were in control during that second (bearish).

A tall body means a large move occurred during the interval; a short body means the price barely shifted between open and close.

### Upper Wick (Session High)

The thin vertical line extending **above** the body is the upper wick. Its tip marks the **highest price** reached during that one-second interval, even if the price retreated before the candle closed.

### Lower Wick (Session Low)

The thin vertical line extending **below** the body is the lower wick. Its tip marks the **lowest price** reached during that interval, even if the price bounced back before the close.

### Color at a Glance

| Color | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 🟢 Green | Price rose — bullish candle |
| 🔴 Red | Price fell — bearish candle |

## Reading Patterns Across Multiple Candles

Individual candles are informative, but sequences reveal momentum.

### Consecutive Green Candles — Uptrend

A run of several green candles in a row suggests sustained buying pressure. Price is consistently closing higher than it opens second after second. This *may* indicate a good moment to hold your shares — or even buy more — though no trend lasts forever.

### Consecutive Red Candles — Downtrend

A run of several red candles signals persistent selling pressure. Price is sliding lower with each passing second. This *may* represent a buying opportunity ("buying the dip"), but be cautious — a crash event can accelerate the decline sharply.

---

## What Wick Length Tells You

### Long Upper Wick — Spike That Retreated

When a candle has a **long upper wick** but a relatively small body, it means the price surged high during the interval but was pushed back down before the close. In Mario Nintendo this often follows a random **+$100 spike event** — price rockets up, then gravity pulls it back. A long upper wick on a recent candle is a warning that the spike may already be over.

### Long Lower Wick — Dip That Recovered

A **long lower wick** with a small body means the price dropped sharply intra-second but buyers stepped in and pushed it back up before the close. This can signal that downward momentum is exhausting itself — a potential reversal point.

---

## Trends Reverse Quickly

Because every candle in Mario Nintendo represents just **one second** of real time, trends are far more volatile than in traditional markets where candles might span minutes, hours, or days. A five-candle green run can flip to red in an instant, especially if a random crash event fires. Never assume a trend will continue — always watch the live price line and the active block for early warning signs.

---

<Tip>
  Keep your eye on the **active block** — the hollow outline on the far right edge of the chart. It shows you the developing candle in real time: watch whether its body is growing green or red, and how long its wicks are getting. This gives you a half-second head start before the candle closes and a new one begins.
</Tip>

<Note>
  Mario Nintendo uses **1-second candle intervals** — dramatically faster than real-world markets where the shortest common interval is typically 1 minute. Patterns that would take hours to develop on a real chart can appear and vanish here in under 10 seconds. Keep that pace in mind when making buy and sell decisions.
</Note>
