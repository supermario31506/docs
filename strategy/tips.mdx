Mario Nintendo drops you into a live market with **$250 cash** and **1 share of SILVER** worth $25 — a net worth of roughly $275 from the start. From there, every second counts. The price fluctuates continuously, rare spike and crash events can swing value by hundreds of dollars in an instant, and there is no safety net. Growing your net worth requires both patience and split-second decisiveness. The tips below will help you do both.

---

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Buy the Dip">
  When you see several consecutive **red candles**, the price is on a downtrend — shares are getting cheaper with every passing second. This is often the best time to spend your cash and acquire more shares at a discount.

  **How to apply it:**
  - Watch for a run of 3 or more red candles in a row.
  - Press **B** (or click Buy) to pick up shares while the price is suppressed.
  - Don't spend *all* your cash — keep a reserve in case the price drops further before recovering.

  The key risk is buying into a crash event that hasn't bottomed yet. Keep an eye on the lower wick length; if candles start showing long lower wicks, the dip may be close to exhausting itself.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Don't Sell All Your Shares">
  It can be tempting to liquidate everything during a strong green run and sit on a pile of cash. Resist that urge. Holding **at least one share** at all times keeps you exposed to the upside of the next move.

  **Why it matters:**
  - A random **+$100 spike** can occur at any frame. If you hold zero shares when it fires, you miss the entire gain.
  - Cash earns nothing while it sits idle; shares can multiply in value within seconds.
  - Re-entering after selling everything means you're racing against the price — and the price often moves faster than you can click.

  Think of your minimum holding as your "exposure floor." Sell down to it during weakness, but never below it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Watch for Spikes">
  Once every ~1,500 frames a **random +$100 spike** fires, briefly sending SILVER's price skyward. On a chart that normally fluctuates by fractions of a dollar, a $100 jump is enormous — it can temporarily **triple or quadruple** the current price.

  **How to take advantage:**
  - When you see a sudden, nearly vertical green candle with a very long body, a spike has likely occurred.
  - This is your window to **sell shares** at a dramatically inflated price.
  - Act fast — the price will drift back down quickly after the spike.

  You usually have only a few seconds at the peak before the price normalizes. Have your finger on the **S** key so you can sell the moment you spot the spike on the chart.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Protect Against Crashes">
  The flip side of spikes: a random **−$200 crash** can fire once every ~3,000 frames, instantly gutting the price. Because crashes are twice as severe as spikes (−$200 vs. +$100), a single crash at the wrong moment can wipe out minutes of accumulated gains.

  **Defensive strategies:**
  - **Sell into strength.** After a significant green run, consider locking in profits by selling some shares. If a crash hits right after, you'll have cash ready to buy the bottom.
  - **Watch for warning signs.** Long upper wicks on recent candles mean the price is already struggling to hold highs — a possible precursor to a reversal or crash.
  - **Never be over-extended.** Spending every dollar of cash on shares right before a crash means you have no buying power at the bottom.

  Remember: the price floor is **$1.00**, so crashes can't send SILVER to zero — but they can still inflict serious damage to your net worth.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Monitor the Net Worth Indicator">
  Your **net worth** (cash + shares × current price) is displayed on screen at all times. It changes color to give you an instant health check:

  - 🟢 **Green** — your net worth is **$250 or more** (at or above your starting cash). You're holding your ground.
  - 🔴 **Red** — your net worth has **fallen below $250**. You're losing ground against your starting cash.

  **How to use it:**
  - Glance at it after every significant market move to orient yourself quickly.
  - If you've been in the red for a while, consider a more aggressive "buy the dip" posture to push your net worth back above $250.
  - If you're deep in the green, you can afford to play more defensively — sell some shares and protect those gains.

  Think of the net worth color as your dashboard warning light: green means you're above $250 and holding your ground, red means it's time to adjust.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Use Keyboard Shortcuts">
  When the price is moving fast — especially during a spike or crash — every millisecond counts. Reaching for the mouse to click a button can cost you a dollar or more in slippage. The keyboard shortcuts eliminate that delay entirely.

  | Key | Action |
  |-----|--------|
  | **B** | Buy one share |
  | **S** | Sell one share |
  | **Q** | Quit the game |

  **Tips for fast trading:**
  - Keep your left hand resting near **B** and **S** at all times so you never have to search for the keys.
  - During a spike, mash **S** repeatedly to unload shares at the peak price across multiple frames.
  - During a dip, hold **B** briefly to accumulate shares quickly before the price bounces.

  Muscle memory with these shortcuts is one of the biggest practical edges you can develop.
</Accordion>
