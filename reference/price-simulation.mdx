The Mario Nintendo simulation engine drives a continuously evolving SILVER price using a combination of frame-by-frame micro-fluctuations and rare, high-impact random events. Every element — from the smooth moment-to-moment price drift to sudden spikes and crashes — is generated procedurally with no connection to real-world market data.

## Normal Price Fluctuation

On every animation frame (approximately 60 times per second), the engine calls `tick_micro_price()`, which applies a small random change of **±0.15%** to the current SILVER price. This constant jitter produces the organic, realistic-looking price movement you see flowing across the chart.

## Random Market Events

Layered on top of the normal fluctuation, each frame also rolls for two rare but dramatic market events:

| Event  | Probability (per frame) | Price Change |
|--------|------------------------|--------------|
| Spike  | 1 in 1,500             | +$100.00     |
| Crash  | 1 in 3,000             | −$200.00     |
| Normal | ~99.93%                | ±0.15%       |

These events fire independently of each other and of the regular micro-price tick, so a spike can theoretically occur on the same frame as a normal fluctuation.

## Price Floor

The SILVER price can never fall below **$1.00**. If a crash or a sustained series of negative ticks would push the price below this threshold, the engine clamps the value to $1.00. This prevents the simulation from reaching an unrecoverable zero-price state.

## Candlestick Blocks (OHLC)

The chart displays price history as **candlestick blocks**, each representing one second of simulation time. The engine's `MarketSimulation` class tracks four values throughout each block and writes them to `price_history` when `finalize_block()` is called at the end of the interval:

| Field     | Meaning                                              |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------------|
| **Open**  | SILVER price at the very start of the 1-second block |
| **High**  | Highest price reached during the block               |
| **Low**   | Lowest price reached during the block                |
| **Close** | SILVER price at the end of the 1-second block        |

A green (bullish) candle is drawn when the close price is higher than the open; a red (bearish) candle appears when the close is lower.

## Chart Auto-Scroll

The chart renders up to approximately **32 candlestick blocks** at a time. Once that limit is reached, older blocks scroll off the left edge and the newest block always appears on the right. The cyan horizontal line overlaid on the chart reflects the **live current price** and updates every animation frame.

<Info>
  Mario Nintendo is a fictional browser game. All price movements, spikes, and crashes are generated by a random number algorithm and do not reflect any real financial instrument, market condition, or trading data.
</Info>
